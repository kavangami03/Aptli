Positioning and messaging
Aptli competes on proof and action, not on a savings headline. Competitors either show discrepancies (reports) or chase them out of view (recovery services); Aptli shows the evidence, decides what is safe to act on, does the work, and counts money only when it arrives.
Primary headline: Hotel revenue reconciliation that proves every dollar and acts on it.
Supporting line: Aptli checks every OTA commission, virtual card and CLC payment against your PMS each day, shows you exactly what your hotel is owed, and then files the dispute, charges the card or posts the payment, with your approval where it matters.
The three-service story
The three services are one daily sequence. Every page that mentions them shows them in this order.
Reconciliation (Reconcile) — matches each reservation across OTA extranets, virtual cards, the CLC portal and the PMS, and flags where records disagree.
Finance Truth Engine (Prove) — classifies each finding, attaches OTA and PMS evidence, excludes authorized exceptions, and tracks the amount from suspected to verified cash.
Automation Action (Act) — files disputes and stay updates, charges eligible virtual cards, syncs CLC check-ins and posts payments, under rules set per property.

 | Design direction
The site should feel like a well-kept ledger, not a startup landing page: calm, exact, and built around real reconciliation rows. The one memorable element is the reconciliation table in the hero; everything around it stays quiet.
Visual idea
The ledger row is the brand device. Three columns — what the OTA or CLC says, what the PMS says, what Aptli did — with a status chip. It appears in the hero, on every solution page’s worked example, and in social graphics.
Highlighter marks the discrepancy. The differing value in a row gets a yellow highlighter underline, like an auditor marking a statement. Use it only on the number that disagrees.
Status chips match the product. Taken care of (green), Needs approval (amber), Your team’s task (blue), Blocked (red). Same names and colors as the daily email and the app.

Typography
One family: Schibsted Grotesk (Google Fonts), weights 400–800, with tabular figures on everywhere so amounts line up.
Scale: H1 36–60 px fluid, H2 28–40 px, H3 21 px, body 17 px, table text 14.5–15.5 px. Line length under 75 characters.
Sentence case throughout; no all-caps labels or eyebrows above headings.
Layout and components
Left-aligned content in a 1,180 px max container; grids collapse to one column below 900 px.
Real HTML tables for comparisons and leak lists (they are indexed and quoted by AI search; images of tables are not). Tables scroll sideways inside their own box on phones.
The Reconcile → Prove → Act sequence is the only place numbered markers are used.
Component list for the design system: header with sticky nav, primary and secondary button, status chip, ledger table, leak table, comparison table, numbered sequence, verified-cash ladder, FAQ accordion (native details/summary), integration logo card, quote block, CTA band, footer.
Imagery: real product screens and the daily email, never stock photos of hotel lobbies or people at laptops. Partner logos only with permission.
Motion
One moment only: on load, the hero table’s verdict chips settle in row by row. No scroll-triggered fades elsewhere. Respect reduced-motion settings.
Accessibility (non-negotiable)
WCAG 2.2 AA: text contrast 4.5:1, touch targets 44 px, visible keyboard focus.
Status is never shown by color alone: every chip carries its words.
Real buttons, links and form labels; one H1 per page; headings in order.
Light and dark mode both designed, following the visitor’s system setting.
SEO, AEO and GEO requirements
The site must rank in Google (SEO), be picked as the direct answer in featured snippets and voice (AEO), and be quoted by ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Claude (GEO). All three reward the same thing: clear, specific, well-structured pages that answer real questions.
Technical SEO (dev + marketing)
Static or server-rendered HTML: every page’s text must be in the HTML, not loaded by JavaScript.
One H1 per page holding the page’s main keyword; H2s phrased as the questions buyers ask.
Unique title (≤ 60 characters) and meta description (≤ 155) per page; canonical URL; Open Graph and Twitter tags with a 1200×630 image.
Clean, lowercase, hyphenated URLs with trailing slash, matching the sitemap section. No URL changes after launch without 301 redirects.
Core Web Vitals targets: LCP under 2.5 s, CLS under 0.1, INP under 200 ms. Fonts self-hosted or preconnected; images in WebP/AVIF with width and height set.
XML sitemap, robots.txt, Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools verified at launch.
Internal links: every solution page links up to its service page and across to the related solutions; every service page links to the homepage CTA.
AEO: answer engine rules (marketing)
Open each section with the answer in 40–60 words, then the detail. The answer must stand alone if lifted out of the page.
Use question H2s (“What is OTA reconciliation?”, “Why do hotels miss virtual card payments?”).
Put comparisons and lists in real HTML tables and lists, not images.
Each page has a 5–8 question FAQ; visible text and FAQ schema must match word for word.
GEO: AI search rules (marketing + dev)
Use the same entity sentence everywhere (homepage, About, LinkedIn, directories, press): “Aptli is a revenue assurance platform for hotels that reconciles OTA commissions, OTA virtual cards and CLC direct-bill payments against the PMS…”
Publish /llms.txt (already drafted) and keep it updated with each new page.
robots.txt explicitly allows GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ClaudeBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended and Applebot-Extended.
Write citable facts: specific, sourced, dated. Name systems and deadlines plainly (Expedia, Booking.com, CLC CheckINN, free-cancellation window).
Earn third-party mentions AI tools rely on: ExploreTECH listing, AAHOA and hotel-tech directories, LinkedIn articles, podcast and trade-press appearances, G2/Capterra profiles once there are reviews.
Add an “Aptli for AI” page (as Evention does) summarising facts for AI assistants.

Content rules, claims and proof
The website must follow the same rule as the product: nothing counts as recovered until it is received. Any claim we can’t prove with our own data stays off the site.
No averaged savings figures (“we save the average hotel $X”) until we have verified-cash numbers from live properties. Use worked examples with real amounts instead, marked as examples.
No certification claims before they are granted. PCI compliance is in progress: say “only the last four digits of a card are stored” until certification is complete.
Integrations: list only live connectors as live; mark others “rolling out” and confirm wording with product before launch.
Third-party names: Expedia, Booking.com, Hotels.com, CLC, Corpay and CheckINN are trademarks of their owners; use names in text, logos only with permission, and keep the trademark line in the footer.
Customer proof: quotes need written approval and a real name, title and property type. Anonymised (“GM, 112-room select-service, Texas”) is acceptable with approval.
Deadlines and OTA rules (reporting windows, dispute cut-offs) must be checked against current OTA partner documentation and dated on the page.
Internal language stays internal: no L0–L4, Exposure, case IDs, engine names, sprint names or client names on the site.
